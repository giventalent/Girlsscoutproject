Understanding the Issue

Breaking the Silence

Sexual abuse of children is one of the most underreported crimes. Understanding it is the first step to stopping it and supporting survivors.

💛

What Is Sexual Abuse?

Sexual abuse includes any sexual activity with a child — touching, exposure, exploitation, or grooming. It is never the child's fault, regardless of circumstances.

🌸

Why Children Don't Tell

Fear, shame, not having the words, threats from the abuser, or worry about not being believed all prevent children from speaking up. Creating safe spaces to share is vital.

💜

The Long-Term Impact

Survivors may experience trauma, anxiety, depression, and difficulties in relationships. With the right support, healing and full, joyful lives are absolutely possible.

🌟

Who Commits Abuse?

Most abusers are known to the child — a family member, trusted adult, or community figure. Abuse can happen in any family, community, or background.

🛡️

Prevention Starts With Us

Education, open conversations with children, and creating environments where they feel safe to speak up are the most powerful tools we have to prevent abuse.

🤝

How to Support a Survivor

Believe them. Listen without judgment. Remind them it's not their fault. Help connect them with professional support. Your response matters more than you know.

"What happened to you was not your fault. Your story matters. Your healing matters. And you deserve every good thing that life has to offer."

Warning Signs

When to Be Concerned

Children often can't tell us directly what's happening. Watch for these signs that a child may need help.

  • Sudden changes in behavior or personality
  • Nightmares, bedwetting, or sleep problems in older children
  • Age-inappropriate sexual knowledge or behavior
  • Withdrawal from friends, family, or activities they used to enjoy
  • Unexplained physical symptoms or pain
  • Fear of certain people, places, or being alone
  • Regression to younger behaviors (thumb-sucking, clinginess)
  • Secrecy with a specific adult, or receiving unexplained gifts
  • Running away or expressing desire not to go home
  • Depression, self-harm, or talk of suicide

If a Child Tells You

Stay calm. Thank them for trusting you. Listen without interrupting. Do not promise secrecy — tell them you will help keep them safe. Do not confront the abuser yourself.

Make a Difference

How You Can Help

Everyone has a role to play in creating a world where every girl is safe. Here's how you can take action.

🗣️

Speak Up

If you suspect abuse, report it. You don't need proof — contact local child protective services or law enforcement. Silence protects abusers, not children.

📚

Educate Children

Teach children about body autonomy, consent, and that no adult should ever ask them to keep secrets. Age-appropriate education is powerful prevention.

💛

Support Survivors

Believe survivors when they share their stories. Listen without judgment. Offer connection to resources, not advice about what they "should" do.

📢

Spread Awareness

Share this page, post on social media, and start conversations. Breaking the stigma and silence around sexual abuse begins with one voice at a time.

🤝

Volunteer & Donate

Support organizations like RAINN, Childhelp, and local crisis centers with your time or resources. They depend on community support to keep services free.

🌱

Create Safe Spaces

Whether at school, in your community, or at home — create environments where children feel safe to speak up, are believed, and are protected.
